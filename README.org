#+TITLE: org-awqat
#+SUBTITLE: Hijri dates, Salah times, Jama'ah tracking and Hijri Org timestamps for Emacs

An Islamic times package for Emacs: plain Elisp, fully offline, no
external binaries.  Part of the Org-on-Android family ([[https://github.com/khaalidrafi/emacshell][emacshell]] +
[[https://github.com/khaalidrafi/elwidget][elwidget]]), works equally on the desktop.

* What it gives you

- *Hijri date* — today's Hijri date, formatting, conversion both ways
  (Emacs' built-in arithmetic Islamic civil calendar).
- *Hijri Org timestamps* — insert, schedule and set deadlines by
  typing a Hijri date; read the Hijri equivalent of any Org
  timestamp.  Org keeps its portable Gregorian timestamps
  underneath, so every other package (agenda, org-habit,
  loophabits...) keeps working; org-awqat is the Hijri front door.
- *Salah times* — Fajr, Sunrise, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib and Isha
  computed offline from standard solar astronomy; several angle
  conventions (MWL, ISNA, Egyptian, Karachi, Umm al-Qura) plus
  manual angles and the Hanafi Asr option.
- *Jama'ah (congregation) times* — your own per-prayer times in a
  plain Org file, with per-weekday overrides (e.g. a different
  Friday schedule).
- *Home-screen widgets* — through the elwidget widget maker.

* Setup

#+BEGIN_SRC emacs-lisp
(use-package org-awqat
  :custom
  (org-awqat-latitude 23.8103)          ; north positive
  (org-awqat-longitude 90.4125)         ; east positive
  (org-awqat-method 'mwl)               ; or 'isna 'egypt 'karachi 'makkah
  (org-awqat-asr-factor 1)              ; 2 for Hanafi
  ;; (org-awqat-timezone-offset 6)       ; nil = auto from your zone
  (org-awqat-jamaah-file "~/org/awqat.org"))
#+END_SRC

* Daily use

| Command | What it does |
|---+---|
| =M-x org-awqat-display-today= | Echo area: today's Hijri + Gregorian date |
| =M-x org-awqat-display-times= | Echo area: today's six Salah times |
| =M-x org-awqat-insert-timestamp-at-point= | Insert an Org timestamp by typing a Hijri date (prefix arg: inactive) |
| =M-x org-awqat-schedule-at-point= | SCHEDULE the entry at point from a Hijri date |
| =M-x org-awqat-deadline-at-point= | DEADLINE the entry at point from a Hijri date |
| =M-x org-awqat-timestamp-hijri-at-point= | Show the Hijri reading of the timestamp at point |
| =M-x org-awqat-new-habit-at-point= | New habit heading starting from a Hijri date |
| =M-x org-awqat-jamaah-set-at-point= | Add/edit one of your Jama'ah times |
| =M-x org-awqat-jamaah-open= | Visit the Jama'ah file for manual editing |

Hijri input everywhere accepts =1447-06-12= or =1447/6/12=; empty
input means today.

* Hijri timestamps, the honest way

Org timestamps are Gregorian internally; pretending otherwise breaks
every other tool that reads your files.  org-awqat therefore does the
conversion at the door: you *think* in Hijri (type it, read it), Org
*stores* Gregorian.  Habits created with =org-awqat-new-habit-at-point=
carry =STYLE: habit= and a =.+Nd= repeater, so [[https://github.com/khaalidrafi/loophabits][loophabits]] and org-habit
track them as usual — while you set and check their dates in Hijri.

* Jama'ah file format

=org-awqat-jamaah-file= (default =~/org/awqat.org=) is ordinary Org:

#+BEGIN_SRC org
,* Default
:PROPERTIES:
:FAJR:     05:30
:DHUHR:    13:15
:ASR:      16:40
:MAGHRIB:  17:55
:ISHA:     19:10
:END:

,* Fri
:PROPERTIES:
:DHUHR:    13:30
:END:
#+END_SRC

The day's own heading wins over =Default=; =M-x
org-awqat-jamaah-set-at-point= maintains the file for you.

* Widgets (Android)

After [[https://github.com/khaalidrafi/elwidget][elwidget]] is set up in the emacshell app:

#+BEGIN_SRC emacs-lisp
(with-eval-after-load 'elwidget
  (org-awqat-widget-register)
  (elwidget-refresh))
#+END_SRC

Then add these on your home screen from the widget picker:

- =awqat-hijri= (2x1) — the Hijri date, with the Gregorian day below
- =awqat-salah= (4x2) — next prayer with countdown + all six times
- =awqat-jamaah= (4x1) — today's Jama'ah times

* org-hijri: Org that natively reads Hijri dates

The second package in this repository, =org-hijri.el=, goes where the
simple conversion helpers stop: Org *displays and edits* Islamic
dates as Islamic, in the file itself.

#+BEGIN_SRC org
DEADLINE: <1448-06-15 Hijri -2d>
<1448-06-15 Hijri 18:30>          ,* active stamp with a time
<1448-06-15 Hijri>--<1448-06-18 Hijri>   ,* date range
<1448-06-15 Hijri +1y>            ,* repeater in Islamic years
#+END_SRC

Only stamps carrying the =Hijri= marker are Islamic; every other
timestamp stays exactly as Org wrote it.  Turn it on globally:

#+BEGIN_SRC emacs-lisp
(use-package org-hijri
  :after org
  :config (org-hijri-mode 1))
#+END_SRC

What the mode does:

- *Parsing* — advice on =org-parse-time-string= (the one choke point
  every timestamp-to-date path in Org funnels through, element parser
  included) converts marked dates to their Gregorian daytime; the
  buffer text stays Hijri, so agenda, deadlines, sorting, clocking and
  comparisons are all exact.
- *The day starts at Maghrib* — =org-hijri-today= rolls over at
  sunset, not midnight (the Hijri analogue of
  =org-extend-today-until=).  Maghrib comes from org-awqat itself
  (your one location setting drives both), or the standalone =awqat=
  package, or =solar.el= as the last resort.  After Maghrib tonight,
  tomorrow's Hijri entries are already "today" in the agenda.
- *Islamic arithmetic* — repeaters step in Islamic months and years
  (~354-day years), and =S-<arrow>= editing
  (=org-timestamp-change=) moves the date inside the Islamic
  calendar, never rewriting your stamp as Gregorian.
- *Agenda* — day headers show each day's Hijri date, and
  =M-x org-hijri-agenda= starts at the current Islamic day.

Commands: =M-x org-hijri-show=, =org-hijri-timestamp=,
=org-hijri-deadline=, =org-hijri-schedule=, =org-hijri-agenda=.

Known limits: date ranges where only one end is marked are parsed but
re-emitted Gregorian on edit; diary sexps ignore the marker; invalid
numerals (day 30 of a 29-day month) keep their Gregorian reading
rather than being guessed at.

* Accuracy notes

- Hijri: the *arithmetic Islamic civil calendar* (Emacs built-in), not
  an observed moon sighting; dates can differ from your local
  declaration by a day.
- Salah: low-precision solar formulas (NOAA-class), accurate to well
  under a minute of angle-driven times; no elevation or atmospheric
  refraction tuning beyond the standard 0.833° horizon depression.
- The angles are conventions, not physics: pick the one your
  community follows.
